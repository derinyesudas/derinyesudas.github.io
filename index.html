  6. 2025

    NVIDIA has committed to 100% renewable energy across its global operations and net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050, while running the data centres that train the models driving its own growth. That tension is the whole question.

    We mapped the company to SDG 9 (industry, innovation and infrastructure) and SDG 13 (climate action), then picked two indicators that could be measured rather than asserted: 9.4.1, CO₂ emissions per unit of value added, and 13.2.2, total annual greenhouse gas emissions.

    Emissions intensity against absolute emissions is the pairing that matters. Intensity alone lets a growing company look greener every year while emitting more. Reading them together is what shows whether innovation is genuinely decoupling the business from its environmental cost.

    A second strand looked at SDG 4, quality education — NVIDIA's Deep Learning Institute, its state education partnerships, and its AI sign-language work — against targets 4.3, 4.4 and 4.5, using Eurostat early-school-leaver data from data.gov.ie.

    Source data came from the UN indicator datasets and NVIDIA's own FY2025 sustainability report, which is the right place to check a company's claims against its own numbers.

  8. 2025

    The dataset held 50,000 customers across 35 features spanning demographics, billing, service usage and support interactions. The target was binary churn, and it was imbalanced: 37,000 customers stayed, 13,000 left.

    Monthly and total charges were mildly right-skewed — a segment paying noticeably more than the rest. Tenure was heavily right-skewed: lots of new customers, few long-standing ones.

    Tenure and total charges correlated strongly, which is expected and therefore not interesting. The RFM score's moderate relationship with usage and support contact was the more useful signal.

    Churn rates across customer segments sat in a narrow band — 25.6% to 26.5% — so segment alone explained almost nothing. Certain plan types and payment methods moved more, pointing at billing and service friction rather than customer type.

    No material missingness, so nothing had to be imputed or dropped.

  9. 2025

    Nous Logic had committed the board to becoming an industry benchmark on sustainability, aligned to SDG 9 and SDG 12, and to reporting under the EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive.

    The problem was not ambition. It was that the data lived in dozens of legacy systems across different departments, countries and formats — so no holistic view of the footprint existed, and nothing could be reported against.

    Our brief as external analytics consultants was a practical framework: a digital sustainability reporting system built on data fabric and analytics technologies, rather than another migration project nobody would finish.

    The work got concrete at the ESRS water metrics, where the reporting standard stops being an abstraction and starts demanding specific numbers from specific systems.

    The piece I found genuinely eye-opening was the ontology — defining the metrics, entities and relationships in a form a machine can read. Sustainability reporting only becomes actionable when the data, the governance and the semantics line up; the ontology is where that happens or does not.

    Three-person team.

  10. 2025

    The waste is not caused by spoiled food. It is caused by a label people misread — “best before” is a quality date, not a safety one, and the words around it (“sell by”, “display until”) make it worse. We benchmarked against Too Good To Go's 2023 and 2024 impact reports.

    Each sprint added one artefact to the Data Value Map, presented in a SHOW and TELL session and revised against the room's feedback: a 2×2 matrix, a benefit matrix, and a data governance map.

    The benefit matrix is structured as start / improve / stop against financial, quantifiable, measurable and observable benefits, with an owner named for every single line — retailers, manufacturers, public health agencies, consumers. A benefit nobody owns does not happen.

    Governance covered the full lifecycle: purchase events logged at creation, raw and aggregated insights stored separately, forecasting and behavioural analytics on top, monitoring that explicitly weighs waste against revenue, and anonymised archival for sustainability reporting. Aligned to UN SDG 12.

    The honest reflection: I came in with no science background and spent day one comparing the Data Value Map to a SWOT analysis just to get a handle on it. I was not confident speaking publicly. Five SHOW and TELL sessions later I was comfortable presenting, and comfortable being the constructive sceptic in the room — which turned out to be the more useful skill.

  14. 2026

    Eight peer-reviewed papers on market manipulation, worked through across five sprints with the pool growing each round.

    The method mattered more than the output. We split into two groups of three; each group coded all eight papers independently before anyone compared notes. The rule was that every paper had to be summed up in one clear phrase before we sat down together.

    We then reconciled where the codes disagreed, settled on one primary code per paper, and fed those into a constant comparative matrix — any pattern that kept recurring became a concept, and each concept became a column.

    The two new papers fell naturally under an existing concept without being forced, which was the signal that the category structure built in earlier sprints was holding.

    Final shape: three concepts under two selective categories — how manipulation works, and the technology used to detect it.

  16. 2026

    The gap we found: Irish whiskey is booming — 16.15 million cases and $5.3 billion globally in 2024, growing at 7.1% a year to 2030 — and not one brand turns its drinkers into creators. Every player in the category innovates at its customers, not with them.

    Ireland is 707,000 nine-litre cases a year and 26% of every spirit sold here. The US alone takes 5.47 million cases — 34% of global Irish whiskey sales. With 40-plus distilleries and 30-plus brands competing for the same shelf, the only differentiator left is who built it with the crowd.

    The model runs Listen → Develop → Release. The NEXUS Lab takes flavour submissions, the community votes, the top five go forward. The Master Blender keeps an absolute quality veto — the crowd inspires, the craft decides. Blind tasting panels with mixologists and the public. Then 3,000 bottles, and the winning contributor's name on every one.

    Go to market: Ireland first, because high whiskey literacy means sharper feedback and validation here travels. Then the US in year two on the 32-million-strong Irish-American diaspora. Then Europe in year three with local co-created editions.

    The financials are deliberately conservative. €55 retail, 64% gross margin on the 3,000-bottle pilot, 68% by year one at 12,000 units and €660,000 revenue, 74% by year three at 55,000 units. COGS falls from €20 to €14.30; marketing drops from 30% of revenue to 15%, because a community-powered brand acquires customers it already owns.

    Every figure above is sourced — Drinks Ireland, IWSR, IWA — and I prepared the Q&A defence for each one, including the ones I expected to be challenged on.

    Most job boards are aggregators of aggregators. This one goes to the source: twelve different applicant tracking platforms — Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable, SmartRecruiters, Recruitee, Personio, Workday, Oracle Cloud, Pinpoint, SuccessFactors and JSON-LD — each with its own reader.

  20. 2026

    The tool parses a job description, scores several CV archetypes against it, recommends the best fit and lets me override. It then selects the profile paragraph, the bullet variants and the ordering of skill terms based on genuine overlap with the ad.

    The part I care about is the safety layer. Four gates run before anything is assembled: a never-claim gate, a dash gate, a provenance gate and a page-fit gate. If a skill is not in the fact bank, it cannot reach the document — which is the whole point, because the fastest way to lose an interview is to be caught claiming something in writing that you cannot do in the room.

    Outputs a CV and cover letter as .docx, a fit-check table showing which terms from the ad actually made it in, pre-populated application-form answers, and a JSON audit trail of every selection made.

    Referee names and salary figures stay in a private local settings file and never enter the generated documents by accident.

    It exists because tailoring a CV by hand for every application is the single biggest time sink in a job search, and doing it badly is worse than not doing it.
